Getting there

How to reach Possotomè

Possotomè lies in the commune of Bopa, Mono Department, in southern Benin. It is reached by road from the coast, with the last kilometres running down to the lakeshore.

From Cotonou

About 1h30 by road via Comè and Bopa, depending on traffic leaving the city. A private car or hired driver is the simplest way and can drop you directly at your hotel.

Shared taxis leave from the city's taxi stations towards Comè or Lokossa. Ask the driver to let you off at the junction for Possotomè, then finish by motorbike taxi. Fares change with fuel prices, so ask locally.

From Lomé (Togo)

Cross the border at Hillacondji, then follow the coastal road east to Comè before turning north towards Bopa. Allow half a day including formalities at the border.

Check your visa requirements for Benin before travelling. Shared taxis run between the border and Comè; from Comè, ask locally for a taxi or motorbike to Possotomè.

From Ouidah or Grand-Popo

Both towns sit on the same coastal road and make an easy day trip. Count around an hour from either, joining the Bopa road at Comè. Possotomè pairs well with a stay on the coast.

Hotels in Ouidah and Grand-Popo can usually arrange a driver for the day. Ask locally for current rates.

The last stretch. From the main road the final kilometres to the lakeshore are on a smaller road that can be rough after heavy rain. Motorbike taxis (zémidjans) are the usual way to cover it if you arrive by shared taxi. Hotels can send someone to meet you if you call ahead.

Best time to visit

When to come

Possotomè is warm all year round. The choice is between the comfortable dry season and the lush, quieter rainy months, and between the three big dates of the yearly agenda.

Dry season, November to March

The most comfortable time to travel: little rain, easier roads and clear evenings on the lake. From December to February the harmattan wind can bring a dusty haze and cooler nights. This is also when the two great ceremonies take place.

Rainy season, roughly April to October

Heavy showers, mostly in the afternoon, with a drier spell around August. The countryside turns green and the lake is full. Allow extra time on the road after storms. August is worth it for the CACE Sunday evenings.

Come for an event

10 January The Royal Ceremony King Ayolomi Laté II gathers the ten villages on the day of the National Vodun Festival.
February Festival Awilé The grand ceremony for Awilé, goddess of Lake Ahémé. The exact date is announced each year.
August, Sundays CACE cultural evenings Poetry, dance and music every Sunday evening in Possotomè Centre.

Dates are indicative. See the full yearly agenda on the Possotomè Life page.

Where to stay

Sleeping by the lake

Three ways to sleep by the lake: a lively ecolodge on the water, the historic hotel that first put the village on the map, and the community-run gîte and campsite of Eco-Bénin.

Ecolodge · Must-visit

Chez Théo les Bains

Almost impossible to miss once you are in Possotomè. Founded and run by Théodore Dotou, this ecolodge sits 50 metres from the shores of Lake Ahémé. Its restaurant is built on stilts over the water, with bungalows in lush gardens, a pool with lake views and thermal baths. Even if you sleep elsewhere, come for a meal on the water.

Historic hotel

Hôtel Village Ahémé

The pioneer of tourism in the arrondissement, just steps from the thermal springs and 1h30 from Cotonou. For decades its 46 rooms on the lakeshore hosted artists and delegations drawn by the Festival Awilé. It has lost some of its shine but remains a witness to a golden era.

Community gîte

Eco-Bénin gîte and campsite

The budget and community option. Possotomè is Eco-Bénin's first eco-village: a gîte for 25 people, a campsite right on the lakeshore and the bar-restaurant Chez Préfet, in a large apatam run by the eco-guide of the same name. On request, guests can help cook local recipes.

Things to do

Days on and around the lake

Possotomè rewards slow travel: a morning in the hot springs, an afternoon on a pirogue, an evening with the community.

Photo courtesy of Lucie Mahé

Our top pick · Agroecology

The Eco-Ferme de Possotomè

A few metres from the village, Ignace Djossa and his father founded this half-hectare farm in 2012. No pesticides, no chemical fertiliser: compost comes from bird droppings, and around 10,000 seedlings of native species leave the nursery every year. Noni, plantain, oranges, mangoes and papayas grow here, some of them turned into juice and jam sold locally.

Ignace trained as a tour guide with Eco-Bénin before turning to agroecology, so a visit is both a farm tour and a walk through the lake's ecosystem. The farm is also a place where young people learn to farm differently, supported by the Mangrove économie initiative of Village Monde and Eco-Bénin.

Visit with Ignace

The thermal springs

Mineral-rich water at over 40°C, the spark that put Possotomè on the map. The same water is bottled under the Possotomé brand and sold across Benin. Chez Théo offers thermal baths on site.

Pirogue trip on Lake Ahémé

Glide out in a wooden pirogue with a local fisherman to see the fish traps, the birdlife and the villages along the shore. Early morning and late afternoon give the best light.

Eco-Bénin circuits

Four guided circuits run by the community: a Vodoun walk through Possotomè, Okomè and Sèhomi (temples, sacred forest, the Sèhomi barter market); about three hours by pirogue on the lake; a half-day hike to the potters' village of Sè with a workshop; and a two-hour medicinal-plants walk. Ask for current rates.

Radio Ahémé FM

The voice of the Mono region, broadcasting in local languages to the lake and the villages. Tune in for the news and event announcements, or ask about visiting the station.

CACE Sunday evenings

Every Sunday evening in August, Possotomè Centre fills with poetry, song, choreography and folkloric performance. Open to all and one of the warmest ways to meet the community. Read more.

The weekly market

Fish from the lake, gari, fruit, cloth and everyday goods. Markets in the region rotate through the week, so ask locally for the market day and go early in the morning.

Respect & etiquette

Being a good guest

Possotomè welcomes visitors warmly. A few simple habits make the welcome mutual.

  • Ask before photographing. Always ask people before taking their picture, and ask the organisers before photographing or filming a ceremony. Some moments are not to be recorded, and a no is a no.
  • Vodun ceremonies are religious events. The Royal Ceremony and the Festival Awilé are acts of worship, not shows. Follow the lead of your hosts, keep your distance from sacred spaces unless invited, and stay quiet when asked.
  • Dress and conduct. Modest, light clothing is appreciated, especially in villages and at ceremonies. Greet people when you arrive somewhere; a simple bonjour opens every door.
  • Bring cash. Card payments are not available in Possotomè. Withdraw CFA francs in Cotonou, Comè or Lokossa before you come, and keep small notes for taxis, the market and the springs.
  • Languages. French is the official language and is understood in hotels and shops. In daily life people speak Sahouè and Pédah, among other local languages. A few words of greeting go a long way.
